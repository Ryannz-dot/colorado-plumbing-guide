Contain the problem first

Turn the main valve clockwise to stop the flow. If the water is spraying from a specific appliance, use the stop valve at that fixture. Open a tap on the lowest level to release pressure, and switch off the water heater if the tank is involved.

Mop or vacuum water up and aim fans at wet drywall and flooring. Keep notes on what got wet. A clear timeline makes the plumber's job easier.

If you can, shut off the water heater's cold supply as well. Letting a tank refill into an open leak makes matters worse.

Clues to look for around the house

  • wet spot in the yard near the street
  • sudden jump in the bill
  • mineral crust on faucets
  • popping noises from the water heater
  • slow drains in several fixtures
  • hissing near the meter

If the meter dial moves when every fixture is off, water is going somewhere. Yard leaks are common between the meter and the house, and a line that crosses clay soil near Jimmy Camp Creek or the Fountain Creek corridor may be stressed by seasonal movement.

A wet patch in dry weather deserves attention, particularly along the line between the street and the home, which is rarely visible.

How the diagnosis tends to go

A plumber will test pressure, inspect visible lines and use listening devices to pinpoint buried leaks. For drains, a camera can reveal root growth or damage. Scale and corrosion at fittings are signs the water in the area is hard on equipment.

If a yard line is suspect, the plumber may mark its path, then dig only where needed. Ask them to explain their reasoning for the dig location.

Sediment in a tank can make it noisy and less efficient, so some plumbers recommend a regular flush. Ask what suits your type of heater.

Repair or replace: the decision points

Whether to repair a spot or replace a section depends on the pipe material, its age and how many failures have already occurred. A single fitting that failed is not the same as a run of pipe that keeps leaking.

Water heaters deserve their own look. A tank that leaks from the body is typically at end of life, while a drip at a connection or relief valve may be a simple fix. Ask the plumber to tell you which applies.

Where a pipe runs through clay that swells and shrinks, fittings take the strain. Replacing a short section with flexible material is sometimes discussed as an option.

Keeping Fountain plumbing healthy

Flush the water heater according to the manufacturer's directions to reduce sediment. Check the pressure reducing valve if your home has one, and watch for pressure spikes.

Keep storm drains clear around the house during summer thunderstorms and monitor sump or low spots near the foundation. Insulate hose bibs and exposed lines before October cold arrives.

Keep a simple file with receipts and notes on repairs. It is useful when you sell, or when the next plumber asks what has been done.

Frequently asked questions

Where does my responsibility start for a yard line?

Generally the homeowner is responsible for the pipe from the meter or property line to the house. Your utility can confirm the exact boundary. Utility rules differ across the Fountain Valley, so check your own bill for the name of the provider and the correct number to call.

Why does my shower pressure fluctuate?

Possible causes include a failing pressure regulator, partial blockage, a worn cartridge or a supply issue. A plumber can narrow it down. Shower pressure that changes by time of day can come from the utility side, not your pipes. Compare notes with a neighbor before assuming the worst.

Is white buildup on faucets a leak?

Not necessarily. It is mineral scale from hard water. Still, a steady drip under the buildup may exist, so check beneath the fixture. To tell scale from a leak, wipe the area dry and watch for fresh moisture over the next hour.

Can I run my sprinklers after a repair?

Ask the plumber. If the repair touched the supply line, the system may need a gradual restart to avoid pressure surges. Irrigation lines are often connected to the house supply, so a careful restart helps prevent a pressure shock.
