Aurora CO Water Leak Repair: A Step-by-Step Homeowner Guide

Stop the water, then document it

Locate the main shutoff valve, often in a basement, utility closet or garage wall, and turn it clockwise until it stops. Open a lower faucet to drain remaining pressure. If the leak is only at one fixture, use the small valve under the sink or behind the toilet instead.

Photograph the damage and note the time you first noticed it. Move rugs and electronics out of the wet area. Those details help the plumber and any later conversation with your insurer, though that is a separate matter from the plumbing itself.

If the valve spins freely without stopping the flow, or leaks around the handle, mention it. The plumber may need to shut the water at the curb stop instead, and that can take a special key.

Hidden leak warning signs

  • warm spot on a floor
  • sound of running water with every fixture off
  • water meter dial moving after shutoff
  • cracked or lifting tile
  • unexplained damp baseboards
  • low pressure in one room only

Homes near Aurora Reservoir, Southlands and the newer communities off E-470 sit on different soil than the older blocks near Colfax Avenue and the Anschutz Medical Campus. Slab-on-grade homes in clay soil can shift slightly, and a stressed pipe under the slab is one reason to check a warm floor early.

Soil that dries out in August and soaks in spring moves a little each year, and rigid pipes do not always forgive it. That is why a new damp patch deserves a call, not a wait-and-see week.

How a plumber usually diagnoses it

The visit often begins with a pressure test of the supply lines and a look at the water meter to confirm that water is actually being lost. From there the plumber may use acoustic detection, thermal imaging or a sewer camera, depending on whether the problem is supply, drain or both.

Finding the leak without opening more of the wall or floor than needed is the goal. Ask the plumber to explain what the equipment found before any repair begins.

Newer pieces of equipment, such as a sewer camera with a locator, let the plumber trace a line from above ground. That often means a smaller opening and a faster conclusion about where to dig.

Repair or replace: what changes the decision

A single failed fitting is usually a spot repair. Several leaks on the same run, pinholes in older copper, or a line under a slab that keeps failing may point toward rerouting or replacing a section instead.

Pipe material matters too. PEX, copper and galvanized steel age differently, and the plumber you are connected with can explain which applies to your house. Ask for the options in plain language, not just a single recommendation.

Bring up your plans for the home. If you intend to sell, remodel or finish a basement, the plumber can suggest what to address now and what can safely wait.

Preventing the next one in Aurora

Check the pressure reducing valve if you have one, and test hose bibs before the first hard freeze. Keep downspouts and sprinklers directing water away from the foundation, because wet clay swells and can push on buried lines.

Know where your sewer cleanout is. Tree roots from mature yards in older parts of Aurora and a flush of grease can both back up a main line, and a quick camera look can prevent a messy surprise.

A short monthly habit works well: look under every sink, glance at the water heater and read the meter once. Five minutes can catch a problem long before a stain appears.

Frequently asked questions

Who do I call about a leak on the street side of my meter?

Usually the water utility handles the main and the service line up to the meter, while the homeowner handles pipe from there into the house. Confirm with your utility, then call if the leak is on your side. Many homeowners call because the meter is spinning and nothing is on. That reading is useful, so keep the shutoff closed until the plumber arrives if you can.

Can a leak be inside a wall without any stain?

Yes, it can. A small pinhole in a copper or PEX line can run for a while before staining appears. A moving meter dial with everything off is a stronger clue than a stain. Soil movement is gradual and the effects vary by lot, so a plumber is well placed to say whether your own pipes are affected.

Do new Aurora subdivisions still get leaks?

They can. Builders use modern materials, but fittings can loosen, pressure can run high, and settling soil can strain connections. Age is not the only factor. If you are unsure what pressure your home runs, a simple gauge on a hose bib will show it. Share the reading with the plumber.

Is a dripping water heater a leak I can ignore?

No. Drips from the tank, the relief valve or its fittings should be checked soon. Shut off the water to the heater and call. A leaking tank typically cannot be repaired in place. A camera inspection is useful before buying a home, too, since it shows the condition of a line you cannot otherwise see.

What if my shutoff valve will not turn?

Do not force it with a tool. A stuck valve can break. Shut the water at the street valve if you have the right key, or ask the plumber how to handle it safely. Meters and shutoffs belong to different parties in different parts of the city, so check your utility's rules before assuming who handles what.
