Your first moves when water shows up

Turn off the main shutoff, then open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to relieve pressure. If you have a finished basement, move belongings off the floor and away from walls.

Next, check whether the water looks like it is coming from pipes or from outside. Clean water appearing near a fixture suggests plumbing. Damp walls after a heavy storm near Boyd Lake or Lake Loveland more often point to drainage around the foundation, which a plumber can help sort out.

If the home is older, mention what you know about the plumbing. A remodeled kitchen with newer copper and a bathroom still on original steel is common, and the plumber needs to know which lines they are touching.

Checklist for spotting a slow leak

  • toilet that runs on its own
  • faucet drip you can hear at night
  • meter that moves with everything off
  • patch of lush grass in winter
  • bubbling paint or wallpaper
  • water bill that climbed without a reason

If you are unsure, take a meter reading before bed and again in the morning with no one using water. A change between the two readings is a good reason to call.

To check the toilet, put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait twenty minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is letting water through, a cheap but constant waste.

What the diagnosis often involves

Plumbers commonly start with the simplest explanations: a worn toilet flapper, a failed supply line, a leaking valve. When those check out, they move to pressure testing, listening devices and thermal imaging for hidden lines.

For drains, a sewer camera can show root intrusion, a belly in the line or a cracked section. Older neighborhoods near Namaqua Park and the downtown core around the Rialto Theater often have clay or cast iron sewer pipe that deserves a closer look.

Take notes about what the equipment shows. Understanding what failed makes it easier to decide whether the repair stands alone or points to wider wear.

Questions to ask before work starts

Ask the plumber what was found, where, and why it failed. Ask whether the repair is local or whether other nearby pipe is in similar shape. Ask how the plumber plans to restore access, such as patching drywall openings or crawlspace panels.

If your house is on a well or part of a rural stretch toward Devil's Backbone Open Space, mention that up front, since the plumbing setup may differ from town service.

A written summary of the work and any recommendations is worth requesting, so you have a record when you sell the house or talk to a future plumber.

Getting ready for winter

Loveland sits at the base of the foothills, where temperature swings can be sharp. Disconnect and drain hoses, shut off sprinkler supply lines, and insulate pipes in garages and crawlspaces.

Travel to Estes Park or Rocky Mountain National Park for a weekend does not mean leaving the house unprepared. Know where the main shutoff is, and ask a neighbor to check on the house if a hard freeze is forecast.

Late-fall cold arrives before many people expect it. Make the checks above part of an October routine and you will rarely face a winter surprise.

Frequently asked questions

Is damp basement wall always a leak?

No. It can be groundwater seeping through the foundation or a drainage problem outside. A quick test is to watch the water meter with everything off. If it does not move, the source may not be a pipe. Groundwater seepage tends to follow rainfall or irrigation, while a plumbing leak continues no matter the weather. That pattern is a handy way to tell them apart.

What is a hose bib and why does it matter in winter?

A hose bib is the outdoor spigot. If a hose is left attached, water can freeze inside the pipe and split it behind the wall. The leak often appears only after thawing. Some yards have an irrigation line feeding the garden from the same supply. Check there too, since a dripping valve box can pose as a plumbing mystery.

Can I replace a toilet fill valve myself?

Many homeowners do, but if the shutoff valve is corroded or the leak persists, a plumber should look. A small leak at the supply can become a larger one. If the shutoff under the toilet weeps, replace it at the same time.

How do I find my sewer cleanout?

Look for a capped pipe, often in the basement, a crawlspace or outside near the foundation. Do not remove the cap if water is backing up, since the line may be full. Cleanouts vary in design and location, so a quick photo of yours will help the plumber identify what you have.
